Emerging Technology

Emerging technology refers to new or developing technologies that have the potential to change how people live, work, communicate, and solve problems.

Examples: Artificial Intelligence (AI), Robotics, Virtual Reality (VR), Augmented Reality (AR), and the Internet of Things (IoT).

Example in Daily Life: AI-powered assistants can answer questions, understand voice commands, and help users perform tasks more efficiently.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a technology that allows computers and machines to learn from data, recognize patterns, understand information, and perform tasks that normally require human intelligence.

Example: AI can recognize faces in photos, translate languages, and recommend videos based on a user's interests.

Machine Learning (ML)

Machine Learning (ML) is a branch of Artificial Intelligence that enables computers to learn from data, identify patterns, and make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed for every task.

Examples or Uses [Mention any 1 or 2 Examples during writing exam]

  1. YouTube: Machine learning algorithms recommend videos based on users' viewing histories and interests.
  2. Email: Machine learning algorithms analyze incoming emails and identify spam messages based on suspicious words, links, and patterns.
  3. Online Shopping: Machine learning systems suggest products based on users' previous purchases, searches, and interests.
  4. Banking: Machine learning systems analyze transaction patterns to detect unusual activities that may indicate fraud.
  5. Healthcare: Machine learning models analyze medical data and images to help doctors identify possible diseases.
  6. Weather Forecasting: Machine learning models analyze past and current weather data to predict future weather conditions.

How Artificial Intelligence (AI) Works

Artificial Intelligence (AI) works by using data, algorithms, and computing power to learn patterns, understand information, and make predictions or decisions. Technologies such as Neural Networks (NN) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) help AI recognize images, understand human language, respond to questions, and solve complex problems.

Common AI Applications

  • Voice Assistants: AI-powered assistants like Siri, Alexa, and Google Assistant understand spoken commands and respond with useful information or perform tasks.
  • Chatbots: AI-powered chatbots on websites answer questions and help customers solve problems.
  • Image Recognition: AI identifies faces, objects, and possible medical conditions from images and medical scans.
  • Recommendation Systems: AI recommends videos or products based on users' interests and preferences. For example, YouTube suggests videos, while Daraz and Amazon recommend products.
  • Self-Driving Cars: AI uses cameras and sensors to understand the surroundings, recognize obstacles, and make driving decisions to help vehicles travel safely.

Benefits of AI in Society

  • Increased Efficiency: AI performs repetitive tasks faster than humans and saves time.
  • Improved Healthcare: AI helps doctors diagnose diseases and predict health problems.
  • Better Agriculture: AI helps farmers monitor crops and improve farming methods.
  • Customer Support: AI chatbots answer customer questions, such as "Where is my order?" for many users at the same time.
  • Better Business Decisions: AI analyses customer behaviour and predicts trends to help businesses make better decisions.
  • Personalized Education: AI provides personalized learning and adaptive tutoring according to students' needs.

Challenges of AI in Society

  • Data Privacy: AI systems may collect and process personal information, creating privacy risks.
  • Job Displacement: AI can automate tasks previously performed by humans, reducing the need for some jobs.
  • Algorithmic Bias: AI may produce unfair results if it is trained on biased or unbalanced data.
  • Ethical Issues: AI decisions can affect people's lives, raising concerns about fairness and responsibility.
  • Lack of Transparency: Some AI systems make decisions that are difficult for people to understand.

Conclusion

AI offers many benefits, but its challenges must be managed carefully. Responsible, fair, and ethical use of AI can help ensure that it benefits society.

Internet of Things (IoT)

Definition: The Internet of Things (IoT) is a technology that connects physical devices to the internet so they can collect, share, and exchange data.

  • Smart Watches: IoT-enabled smartwatches collect information such as heart rate and physical activity and share it with connected applications.
  • Smart Cities: IoT sensors monitor traffic and streetlights to improve traffic management and save energy.

  • Smart Homes: IoT connects devices such as smart lights, air conditioners, and security cameras to the internet. Users can control these devices using their smartphones, even when they are away from home.
  • Healthcare: IoT connects devices such as smartwatches and health monitors to the internet. These devices measure health information, such as heart rate and blood pressure, and share it with doctors to help them monitor patients' health remotely.
  • Agriculture: IoT uses sensors to monitor soil moisture, temperature, and crop conditions. It helps farmers decide when to water crops, check their growth, and estimate the right time to harvest. This helps farmers save water, improve crop production, and manage their fields more effectively.

Data Collection, Communication and Automation

How IoT Works

IoT works through three main processes:

  1. Data Collection: IoT devices use sensors to collect different types of information from their surroundings, such as temperature, humidity, movement, air quality, soil moisture, and health information.
  2. Communication: IoT devices send the collected data to other devices, computers, or applications through communication networks, such as the internet or Bluetooth.
  3. Automation: IoT devices use the collected data to perform tasks automatically with little or no human involvement, making daily activities easier and more efficient.

Example: In smart agriculture, IoT sensors collect information about soil moisture and send it to a control system. If the soil becomes too dry, the system automatically turns on the water pump to water the crops.

Cloud Computing

Definition: Cloud computing is a technology that allows users to store data, use software, and access computing services over the internet instead of relying only on their own computers.

Example

  • Online Storage: Google Drive and Dropbox allow users to store files online and access them from anywhere with an internet connection.

Examples from Textbook

  • Amazon Web Services (AWS): AWS provides cloud services that allow users and businesses to store data, run applications, and use computing resources over the internet.
  • Microsoft Azure: Microsoft Azure provides cloud services for storing files, hosting websites, running applications, and managing data over the internet.

Advantages of Cloud Computing

  • Scalability: Cloud computing allows users to increase or decrease storage and computing resources according to their needs.
  • Accessibility: Users can access their files, applications, and data from different devices and locations through the internet.
  • Cost-Effectiveness: Cloud computing reduces the need to buy expensive hardware and maintain physical servers, helping users and businesses save money.

Blockchain Technology

Definition: Blockchain is a technology that stores information in blocks connected in a chain. The information is shared across multiple computers, making it difficult to change or delete recorded data without detection.

Examples or Uses [Mention any 1 or 2 Examples during writing exam]

  1. Cryptocurrency Transactions: Blockchain is used to send and receive digital currencies such as Bitcoin and Ethereum without relying on a traditional bank to record each transaction.
  2. Banking and Finance: Some banks and financial companies use blockchain to transfer money, settle payments, and maintain secure transaction records.
  3. Supply Chain Management: Companies use blockchain to track products from manufacturers to shops and check where products come from.
  4. Digital Identity: Blockchain can be used to verify identities and help protect digital identity records.
  5. Healthcare: Hospitals and healthcare organizations can use blockchain to help maintain secure medical records and track access to information.
  6. Smart Contracts: Blockchain can run programs called smart contracts that automatically perform agreed actions when specific conditions are met.
  7. Bitcoin: Bitcoin is a digital currency that uses blockchain technology to record and verify transactions between users without requiring a traditional bank to process them.

Cultural and Ethical Implications of AI Systems

AI systems can affect people's culture, values, privacy, and daily lives. Therefore, AI should be developed and used fairly, responsibly, and respectfully.

Stakeholders in AI Systems

  • Developers: Developers design and create AI systems. They should make sure AI works fairly, protects users' data, and does not produce biased results.
  • Users: Users interact with AI systems in their daily lives. They should use AI responsibly and be careful when sharing personal information.
  • Government: Governments make laws and rules to ensure AI is used safely, protects people's rights, and prevents misuse.
  • Communities: Communities are groups of people affected by AI systems. AI should respect their cultures, traditions, and values and should not unfairly discriminate against any group.

Cultural Awareness and Diversity

Cultural awareness and diversity means understanding and respecting the different cultures, languages, traditions, and beliefs of people.

AI systems should respect different cultures and languages and treat people fairly. They should be designed to work well for people from different backgrounds without showing unfair preferences toward any group.

Example

AI Translation: An AI translation system should support different languages so that people from different countries can communicate easily.

Ethical Principles in AI

Fairness, Accountability, Transparency

  • Fairness: AI systems should treat all people equally and should not discriminate against anyone based on their gender, culture, or background.
  • Accountability: Developers and organizations should take responsibility for the decisions and actions of their AI systems.
  • Transparency: AI systems should provide clear information about how they work and how they make decisions.

Avoiding Bias and Ensuring Inclusivity Design

AI systems should be designed to work fairly for people from different backgrounds, languages, and abilities. Developers should reduce bias so that no group is treated unfairly.

Conflicts of Interest Among Stakeholders

Definition: Conflicts of interest occur when different stakeholders have different goals or interests regarding the development and use of AI systems.

Examples or Conflicts [Mention any 1 or 2 Examples during writing exam]

  • Developers vs. Users: Developers may want to collect more data to improve AI, while users want to protect their personal information.
  • Businesses vs. Communities: Businesses may use AI to reduce costs, but this may lead to job losses in communities.
  • Government vs. Users: Governments may use AI for security and monitoring, while users want to protect their privacy.
  • Businesses vs. Government: Businesses may want fewer restrictions on AI, while governments want rules to ensure its safe and fair use.

Importance: These conflicts should be managed carefully to ensure that AI benefits society while respecting people's rights, privacy, and interests.

Policy and Legal Frameworks

Definition: Policy and legal frameworks are rules and guidelines that help ensure AI is developed and used safely, fairly, and responsibly.

Examples or Frameworks

  1. GDPR: The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) protects personal data and gives people rights over how their information is collected and used.
  2. AI Ethics Guidelines: These guidelines encourage fairness, transparency, accountability, and respect for human rights when developing and using AI systems.
  3. National AI Policies in Pakistan: These policies help promote AI research, protect data, and encourage the responsible use of AI according to local needs.

5. What is Blockchain technology? Discuss how its decentralized nature improves transparency and security in financial and non-financial applications.

How Blockchain Improves Security and Transparency

  • Decentralization: Records are shared across multiple computers instead of being controlled by a single central authority.

  • Transparency: Participants can view and verify transactions recorded on the blockchain.

  • Digital Records: Blockchain can maintain verifiable records of important information, such as certificates and documents.

Applications of Computer Science FAQs

Emerging technology refers to new or developing technologies that have the potential to change how people live, work, communicate, and solve problems. Examples include Artificial Intelligence (AI), Robotics, Virtual Reality (VR), and the Internet of Things (IoT).

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a technology that enables computers and machines to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as recognizing images, understanding language, and making predictions.

Machine Learning (ML) is a branch of Artificial Intelligence that enables computers to learn from data, identify patterns, and make predictions or decisions without being explicitly programmed for every task.

AI uses data, algorithms, and computing power to learn patterns, process information, and make predictions or decisions. Technologies such as Neural Networks and Natural Language Processing help AI recognize images and understand human language.

Common AI applications include voice assistants such as Siri and Alexa, chatbots, image recognition, recommendation systems, and self-driving cars. These applications help automate tasks and improve efficiency.

AI improves efficiency, healthcare, agriculture, customer support, business decisions, and personalized education. However, it also creates challenges such as data privacy risks, job displacement, algorithmic bias, ethical concerns, and lack of transparency.

The Internet of Things (IoT) is a technology that connects physical devices to the internet so they can collect, share, and exchange data. Examples include smartwatches, smart home devices, and agricultural sensors.

IoT works through three main processes: data collection, communication, and automation. Sensors collect information, connected devices transmit the data, and control systems can use it to perform tasks automatically.

In healthcare, IoT devices monitor health information. In agriculture, sensors measure soil moisture and crop conditions. In smart homes, connected lights, air conditioners, and security cameras can be monitored and controlled remotely.

Cloud computing is a technology that allows users to store data, run applications, and access computing services over the internet instead of relying only on their own computers. Examples include Google Drive, Amazon Web Services (AWS), and Microsoft Azure.

The main advantages of cloud computing are scalability, accessibility, and cost-effectiveness. It allows users to adjust computing resources, access files from different locations, and reduce the need to purchase and maintain physical servers.

Blockchain is a technology that stores information in blocks connected in a chain. Records can be distributed across multiple computers, and cryptographic methods help protect information and make unauthorized changes easier to detect.

Financial applications include cryptocurrency transactions and banking services. Non-financial applications include supply chain management, digital identity verification, healthcare records, and digital certificates.

The main ethical principles in AI include fairness, accountability, and transparency. Fairness helps prevent discrimination, accountability ensures that responsible parties take ownership of decisions, and transparency makes AI systems easier to understand.

Cultural awareness helps AI systems respect different languages, traditions, beliefs, and backgrounds. Legal frameworks, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), establish rules to protect personal data, privacy, and people's rights and encourage responsible technology use.
